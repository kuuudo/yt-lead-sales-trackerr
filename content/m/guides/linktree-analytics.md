You put a Linktree in your Instagram and TikTok bios. Its analytics page now shows views, clicks and a click rate. What it does not show is whether any of those clicks turned into a lead, a call or a sale.

This guide explains what Linktree analytics reports, how much history each plan keeps, where its answers stop, and how to connect bio-link clicks to revenue. Everything we say about Linktree's features comes from Linktree's own help center as of November 2026. Plans change, so check its current pages before you decide anything. We make VSTRK, a tool for tracking content to leads and revenue, so we mention it where it fits. Much of this guide works without any extra tool, and we say where a different approach is the better choice.

These numbers are good at judging the page itself. You can see which link on the page gets clicked, whether your click rate drops after you rearrange things, and which kind of source sends visitors. Linktree's own example makes the click rate concrete: 1,000 clicks on 2,000 views is a 50% click rate.

Notice what click rate measures. It divides clicks by views, so it tells you how engaging the page is. It does not tell you whether the people who clicked went on to buy.

Linktree's paid-features overview adds a few things that matter for tracking:

- **UTM parameters.** On the Pro and Premium plans, you can have your links include UTM parameters so that Google Analytics can tell where traffic came from.

If you are on a plan with a short window, do not rely on Linktree to remember last quarter for you. Copy your key numbers into your own sheet once a month.

## What Linktree analytics cannot tell you

Linktree's analytics is built to describe a link-in-bio page. Based on the measures Linktree documents, these are the questions it does not answer:

- **Which post sent the click.** Sources are described at the level of channels or platforms. If you promote from ten Reels and one bio link, every visit can land under the same platform, and you cannot tell the Reels apart.
- **Whether a click became a lead, call or purchase.** The documented measures stop at views, clicks and Linktree's own subscribers. A booking, a checkout or a payment on your own site or in your payment processor is not on that list.
- **Revenue per link, per post or per platform.** Without the step above, there is nothing to attach a dollar amount to.
- **How a person moved between platforms.** Someone who finds you on TikTok, follows you on Instagram and buys after your newsletter appears as separate, unconnected visits.
- **Clean numbers.** Linktree's help center says your own test clicks are counted and that clicks cannot be reset. It also says new clicks can take up to 30 minutes to appear.

None of this is a flaw in Linktree. A bio-link page is one link shared by every post, so the data cannot separate the posts. That is the gap you have to close yourself.

## Three ways to get from clicks to revenue

These setups differ in cost, effort and how far down the funnel they reach.

### Linktree's paid integrations plus Google Analytics

If you are on the Pro or Premium plan, you can add UTM parameters to your links and send Linktree events to Google Analytics. You then see sessions by source and campaign in a tool you may already use, and you can record events for the goals you care about. This is a good fit when your priority is traffic quality and on-site behavior. The effort comes later: payments still live in your payment processor, so turning events into dollars means connecting them. Our [UTM tracking guide](/m/guides/utm-tracking-guide/) explains how to name the parameters so the data stays clean.

### Tagged links for each post, plus form fields or a CRM

Wherever a platform lets you place a clickable link, such as a video description, a newsletter or some posts, give that piece of content its own tagged link instead of sending everyone through the bio link. You can build the links with our free [UTM Link Builder](/m/tools/utm-link-builder/). Then add hidden fields to your signup and booking forms so each lead is stored with its source. This is a strong fit when your sales process already runs through a CRM. Your bio link stays useful for the places that only allow one link, and it takes some setup to match payments recorded in another system.

### An attribution tool that joins clicks, conversions and payments

The third option is a tool whose whole job is connecting the click to the outcome and to the money. To do that, a tool has to capture the source at the moment of the click, record conversion events, tie the events to the same visitor over time, and pull in real payment data. ## Getting cleaner data from Linktree today

Whatever you decide, these habits improve the numbers you already have:

- **Do your testing early and write down the count.** Test clicks are included and clicks cannot be reset, so note your own clicks or test before you start comparing.
- **Wait before judging a change.** New clicks can take up to 30 minutes to show up, so do not conclude anything from the first few minutes.
- **Watch click rate as a trend.** Compare it before and after you reorder or rename links, not against someone else's number.
- **Save your own monthly snapshot.** Windows are 28 days on Free and 90 days on Starter, so record views, clicks and top links in a sheet each month.
- **Use one naming convention for UTM values.** If you use UTM parameters, keep every value lowercase and consistent, as described in the [UTM tracking guide](/m/guides/utm-tracking-guide/).
- **Point each campaign at its own destination.** If one bio link carries several offers, the click on one link tells you little about which offer a post sold.

## Common mistakes

- Treating click rate as a sales metric. It is clicks divided by views.
- Comparing platforms by views when you care about customers.
- Counting your own test clicks as audience interest.
- Assuming a longer history is available when your plan only keeps 28 or 90 days.
- Sending every post to the same link, then trying to work out afterwards which post sold.
- Changing UTM naming mid-campaign, so old and new data cannot be compared.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Linktree analytics free?

Linktree's help center says the Free plan can view analytics for the last 28 days. Longer ranges depend on the plan. Plans and limits change, so check Linktree's current help page.

### Can Linktree show which post sent a click?

Linktree describes its sources as the channels or platforms visitors used, such as direct visits, search engines or social media. It does not describe post-level sources. To separate posts, give each post its own tagged link where the platform allows it.

### Can I use UTM parameters with Linktree?

Linktree's paid-features overview says the Pro and Premium plans let you have your links include UTM parameters. See our [UTM tracking guide](/m/guides/utm-tracking-guide/) for naming conventions.

### Does Linktree work with Google Analytics?

Linktree's help center says its Google Analytics integration lets you track Linktree events in your own Google Analytics account, and its paid-features overview places that integration on the Pro and Premium plans.

### Can Linktree show revenue?

The analytics measures Linktree documents are views, clicks, click rate, subscribers, sources, locations and devices. A payment made on your own site or through your payment processor is not among them. To see revenue by post or platform, you need to match each payment to the link that started the visit, either in your own reporting or with a tool built for that.

### Do my own clicks count in Linktree analytics?

Yes. Linktree's help center says your own test clicks are included, and that new clicks can take up to 30 minutes to appear.

### Can I export my Linktree analytics?

Linktree's help center documents a CSV download for analytics on the Premium plan. On other plans, record your key numbers in your own sheet.

## Conclusion

Linktree analytics is a reasonable way to see how your link-in-bio page performs: what gets viewed, what gets clicked and which kinds of sources send people. It is not designed to tell you which post produced a lead or which click produced a sale, because one shared link cannot separate your posts.

To answer those questions, give each piece of content its own tagged link where you can, record what visitors do after the click, and connect payments back to the link that started the journey. Linktree with Google Analytics, tagged links with a CRM, and a dedicated attribution tool are three ways to get there.
