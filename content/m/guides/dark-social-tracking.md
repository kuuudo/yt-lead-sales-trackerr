Dark social is sharing that your analytics cannot see: links passed around in private messages, group chats, text messages and forwarded emails. The people who click those links still visit your site, but the visit usually shows up as "direct" traffic, with nothing pointing back to the post, video or email that started it.

You cannot measure all of it. You can measure far more of it than most people do, and you can stop giving the wrong content credit for the results. This guide explains what dark social is, how to tell whether it affects you, five practical ways to measure it, and where the limits are.

## What dark social is

The term was coined in 2012 by Alexis Madrigal, then at The Atlantic. It has nothing to do with the dark web, and it is not a type of advertising. It simply describes sharing that happens out of sight of your analytics. Typical places include:

- Messaging apps and text messages
- Direct messages inside social apps
- Private group chats
- Emails forwarded from one person to another

When someone clicks a plain link in one of these places, your analytics often has no referring site to read, so it files the visit under direct. Direct traffic also includes people who type your address or use a bookmark, so "direct" and "dark social" are not the same thing. Dark social is one of the things hiding inside the direct bucket.

## Why it matters for revenue

A link sent by a friend or colleague often carries more trust than a link someone finds on their own. If those visits are filed as direct, two things go wrong. The content that started the conversation gets no credit, and the report points you toward whatever the buyer touched last.

**Example:** You publish a YouTube video. A viewer sends it to a business partner in a chat. The partner visits your site the next day and books a call. Your report shows one direct visit and one booking. The video that started it all does not appear.

## How to tell dark social is affecting you

Look for these signs in your reports:

- **Direct traffic landing on deep pages.** People rarely type a long address by hand. Direct visits to a specific article or offer page often came from a shared link.
- **Direct spikes after you publish.** If direct visits rise on the days you post, some of them likely came from that post.
- **Customers who say "a friend sent me"** while your reports show nothing that explains it.

## Five ways to measure more of it

| Method | What it catches | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A tracking link on everything you publish | Forwarded links that still carry your tag | Misses plain page addresses copied from a browser |
| A separate link per partner or creator | Clicks by person, including links they re-share | Only covers people you gave a link to |
| "How did you hear about us?" on forms and calls | Sources no tool can see | Relies on memory and honest answers |
| Promo or referral codes | Offers passed along in private chats | Only counts people who use the code |
| Reading direct traffic by page and date | Likely shared content | An estimate, not proof |

### 1. Put a tracking link on everything you publish

Tag every link in your posts, video descriptions, bio and newsletter. If a reader forwards that exact link, the tag travels with it, and the click is credited to the post that started the chain. Build tagged links with the free [UTM link builder](/m/tools/utm-link-builder/), and see the [UTM tracking guide](/m/guides/utm-tracking-guide/) for naming rules. Platform-specific details are in the [Facebook UTM guide](/m/guides/facebook-utm-parameters/) and the [Instagram tracking guide](/m/guides/track-instagram-post-and-reel-to-revenue/).

### 2. Give each partner or creator their own link

If affiliates, creators or team members share your offer, give each one a separate [partner tracking link](/m/features/partner-tracking-links/). When one of them forwards it in a private message, the click still belongs to them.

### 3. Ask the question

Add "How did you hear about us?" to signup forms, checkout and booking pages, and ask it on sales calls. Use an open text field rather than a fixed list. A list only contains the sources you already know about, and dark social is mostly made of the ones you do not.

### 4. Use promo or referral codes for private channels

A code is something a person can type into a message. A creator can mention "use code FRIEND" in a video, and someone can pass it on in a chat. Use a different code for each creator, video or campaign so you can tell them apart.

### 5. Read direct traffic by page and date

Compare direct visits to your posting calendar. Which pages get direct visits that have no obvious source? Which days spike? This does not prove where a visit came from, but it tells you which content is probably being shared privately.

## What no tool can do

No software can attribute every private share. If a tool claims it can, treat that claim with caution. Some older dark social tools were built around share buttons and code snippets that tried to catch copied links, but they only covered some cases. Treat your results as a floor, not a total: the real number is higher, and you cannot see the full amount.

Also remember that a forwarded tagged link keeps its original tag. That is the right credit for the content that started the chain, but it means a channel's click count includes people who never saw the original post.

## FAQ

### Is dark social the same as direct traffic?

No. Direct traffic is a label in your analytics. Dark social is one of the things that ends up under that label, along with typed addresses and bookmarks.

### Can Google Analytics track dark social?

Not directly. It cannot see inside a private chat. It can show you direct traffic landing on deep pages, and it can credit tagged links you created yourself.

### Do UTM parameters solve dark social?

Only for links you tagged yourself, and only when the tagged link is the one that gets forwarded. A plain page address copied from the browser carries no tag.

### Does "dark" mean the content is hidden or illegal?

No. It only means the sharing happens where your analytics cannot see it. Sending a link to a friend in a chat is ordinary behavior.
