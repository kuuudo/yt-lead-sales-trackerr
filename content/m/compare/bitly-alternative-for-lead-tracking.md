# VSTRK vs Bitly

Bitly and VSTRK both put a short link on your content, but they answer different questions. Bitly is built to create, shorten, brand, manage and measure links. VSTRK is built to show which pieces of content, which promoters and which promotional paths lead to leads, calls, purchases and revenue. If you only need to know how many people clicked a link, Bitly is a mature, capable choice. If you need to know which of your videos, partners or campaigns produced business results, that is the gap VSTRK is designed to fill.

We make VSTRK, so we have tried to be specific about where Bitly is stronger and to mark anything we could not confirm. Bitly's capabilities come from Bitly's own pages as of November 2026, and VSTRK's come from our product. Where a feature was not established in the sources we reviewed, the table says so instead of assuming it is missing.

**The short version**

- **Choose Bitly** if your main job is creating and managing short links, branded domains, QR codes and campaign links, and measuring clicks and scans by location, device and referrer.
- **Choose VSTRK** if you publish content on social platforms or work with other promoters, and you need individual content and partner results tied to leads, sales calls, purchases and Stripe revenue.
- **Use both** if you need print-ready QR codes and geo-routing for one campaign and outcome tracking for another. They solve different problems.

## Where Bitly excels

Bitly is not a basic URL shortener, and it would be a mistake to treat it as one.

- **Link management at scale.** Branded short links with custom domains and back-halves, redirects, and bulk shortening. If you manage large numbers of links, this is Bitly's home ground.
- **QR codes.** Dynamic QR codes you can update after printing, with custom colors, patterns and logos, several download formats and bulk creation. VSTRK does not offer a comparable feature that we can point to.
- **Geographic, device and referrer analytics.** Bitly reports where clicks and scans come from, what devices people use and which referrers send them, with filters by campaign and time period. Bitly describes the location data as aggregated rather than tied to individuals.
- **Dynamic routing.** One link or QR code can send visitors to different destinations by country, region, device type or platform, and can fall back to another page after a campaign ends.
- **Landing pages and link-in-bio.** Bitly includes simple mobile-friendly landing pages and link-in-bio pages.
- **UTM and campaign tracking.** A UTM builder and campaign-level reporting, so link traffic can be connected to downstream results in tools like Google Analytics.
- **Integrations and API.** Bitly lists a large integration catalog, including Shopify, Canva, Zapier and a public API, and offers a HubSpot connection for Marketing Hub customers. The scale and maturity of that ecosystem is a real advantage.
- **Entry price.** A free plan, and paid plans starting at $10 per month with annual billing.

None of this is minor. For print campaigns, packaging, QR-led marketing and brand-controlled links, Bitly is likely the better tool.

## Why link clicks aren't the same as revenue

A click shows that someone was interested enough to follow a link. It does not show whether they signed up, booked a call, paid, or left. Bitly's own analytics documentation is direct about this: its analytics stop at engagement with the link, and outcomes such as conversions, purchases, donations and revenue are measured elsewhere, usually in web analytics platforms, CRMs or ecommerce tools.

That is a fair and reasonable division of labor, and Bitly supports it through UTM parameters. A typical do-it-yourself setup looks like this:

1. Create a link for each piece of content and tag it with UTM parameters.
2. Send visitors to your site, where a web analytics tool records the visit and the campaign tags.
3. Set up events for the outcomes you care about, such as signups and purchases.
4. Connect your payment data to that analytics tool so purchases carry a revenue value.
5. Build reports that join all of it together.

This works, and many teams run it well. The effort sits in steps 2 to 5, and so do the common failure points: inconsistent tag names, conversions that don't carry the campaign through, and payment data that doesn't reach the analytics tool. Google's own guidance notes that UTM values are case sensitive, so `TikTok` and `tiktok` show up as separate rows.

VSTRK takes the opposite approach. It starts from the outcome. Each piece of content or each promoter gets a tracking link, VSTRK records clicks and conversion events such as newsletter signups, leads, consultations, sales calls and purchases, and with Stripe connected it ties payments to the tracking data. The aim is that "which video or partner produced this sale?" is a question you answer in one place.

Neither approach is perfect. Both depend on people clicking a tracked link. Someone who watches a video, searches for your name a week later and buys will not show up as coming from the video in either system.

## Tracking individual videos and content

Imagine you publish 20 videos across Instagram, TikTok and YouTube, and each links to the same sales page.

**Illustrative example. These are hypothetical numbers, not data from any real account.** Assume each purchase is $50.

| Video | Platform | Clicks | Consultations booked | Purchases | Revenue from purchases | Revenue per click |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Video 03 | TikTok | 420 | 0 | 2 | $100 | $0.24 |
| Video 07 | YouTube | 180 | 4 | 9 | $450 | $2.50 |
| Video 12 | Instagram | 260 | 1 | 3 | $150 | $0.58 |
| Video 18 | YouTube | 90 | 3 | 6 | $300 | $3.33 |

**What Bitly gives you.** If you create a separate short link for each video, Bitly shows how many clicks each one received, where they came from and on what devices. In this example it would rank Video 03 first with 420 clicks, and Video 18 last with 90.

**What outcome tracking adds.** Ranked by revenue per click, the order reverses. Video 18 brought the fewest visitors but the most value per visitor, and Video 03 brought the most visitors and the least. If you judged only by clicks, you would make more videos like Video 03.

To get that second view with Bitly, you tag each link with UTM parameters, record conversions in an analytics tool, and connect payments. In VSTRK, each piece of content gets its own tracking link, and clicks, conversions and Stripe revenue are reported against it, so the content-level comparison comes with the link.

Two practical points apply either way. Each video needs its own link or identifier, because a link reused across several videos can tell you the link worked but not which video did the work. And with only a few hundred clicks per video, treat differences as signals to investigate, not final verdicts.

## Tracking multiple promoters and partners

Now imagine five promoters, such as affiliates, creators or agency staff, promoting the same sales page of a $100 service.

**Illustrative example. These are hypothetical numbers, not data from any real account.**

| Promoter | Platform | Clicks | Consultations booked | Purchases | Revenue | Revenue per click |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Promoter 1 | YouTube | 300 | 6 | 4 | $400 | $1.33 |
| Promoter 2 | TikTok | 520 | 2 | 3 | $300 | $0.58 |
| Promoter 3 | Instagram | 150 | 5 | 5 | $500 | $3.33 |
| Promoter 4 | LinkedIn | 90 | 3 | 2 | $200 | $2.22 |
| Promoter 5 | X | 210 | 0 | 1 | $100 | $0.48 |

Promoter 2 sent the most traffic and produced less revenue than three of the others. Promoter 3 sent the fewest visitors but the most revenue. Pay or reward by clicks and you reward the wrong person.

**With Bitly,** you can create five separate short links to the same page and compare clicks, referrers, devices and locations per link. That is a legitimate way to see who sends traffic. It does not, on its own, tell you who produced consultations or purchases. For that you need UTM tags per promoter, an analytics tool and a way to connect payments.

**With VSTRK,** partner tracking is part of the product. Multiple promoters promote the same asset, such as a sales page, newsletter, consultation page or direct purchase link, each with a separate tracking link. VSTRK supports a custom tracking domain, so you can share an address such as `go.yourdomain.com` with your promoters, and they can generate their own links under it, like `go.yourdomain.com/1234`. You then compare the results each person generates: clicks, signups, leads, consultations, sales calls, purchases and revenue.

One caution applies to every tool. When more than one promoter appears in a single customer's path, someone has to decide who gets credit. Agree that rule with your partners in writing before you pay anyone, and check how your tool reports those journeys.

## Tracking leads, calls and purchases

Different outcomes matter to different businesses, and they should not be blended into one number.

| Outcome | What it means | Why it should be tracked separately |
|---|---|---|
| Newsletter signup | Someone joins your list | A soft signal; not paid revenue |
| Lead | Someone shows buying intent, for example by submitting a form | Quality varies widely by source |
| Consultation or sales call | Someone books time with you | Often the step before a high-value sale |
| Purchase | Someone pays | The outcome most businesses ultimately care about |

**Bitly's route.** Bitly's analytics report engagement with the link. Outcomes come from other systems. You can tag links with UTM parameters and read results in a web analytics tool, or connect Bitly to other tools, for example through Zapier or a HubSpot integration. Bitly describes the HubSpot connection as tying link engagement to contact records, with branded short links used in HubSpot content. Bitly also says it can connect to revenue outcomes, but its page does not explain how revenue is measured, so we have not relied on that claim.

**VSTRK's route.** VSTRK records newsletter signups, leads, consultations, sales calls and purchases against the content or promoter link that preceded them. With Stripe connected, payments are tied to the tracking data. Newsletter signups are tracked as conversions but are not counted as paid revenue, so a signup does not inflate your revenue totals.

Whichever route you take, test it end to end before you rely on it: click your own link, complete a test signup or a test purchase, and confirm the result appears where you expect it.

## Cross-platform customer journeys

Few customers see one post and buy. A more realistic path looks like this:

**X post → TikTok video → YouTube video → sales page → purchase**

Link analytics show you each link's clicks and which referrers sent them. That is useful, but it reports each link separately. It does not, as far as Bitly's published materials establish, join those links into one path for a single customer, so the question "what did the customer see before buying?" has to be answered with other tools.

VSTRK is built around that question. You can connect pieces of content across platforms into one journey, including branches where one piece of content leads to several next steps, and see clicks, conversions and conversion rates at each step. A journey can also cross from one domain to another, including your own subdomains. If a YouTube video isn't converting or people drop off before booking a call, you can see where.

Some limits are worth stating plainly:

- A touchpoint shows up only if it passed through a tracked link.
- People who never click a tracked link cannot be tracked back to the content that influenced them.
- No link-based method guarantees perfect tracking across devices and browsers.
- Tracking touchpoints is different from deciding which touchpoint gets credit for a sale. Decide how you want to credit them, and check how your tool reports it.

## Where Bitly is the better fit

Choose Bitly when:

- **You need QR codes.** For print, packaging, events or signage, Bitly's dynamic QR codes are a clear strength.
- **You need branded links and link governance.** Custom domains, back-halves, redirects and bulk management are core to Bitly.
- **You need geographic or device data, or smart routing.** Bitly reports it and can route visitors by country, region, device or platform.
- **You want a simple, low-cost start.** The free plan and low-priced tiers are hard to beat for basic link needs.
- **Your analytics already live elsewhere.** If your team has a web analytics setup, a CRM and an ecommerce reporting process, Bitly plus UTM tags fits neatly into it.
- **You need a large integration layer or an API.** Bitly's catalog and developer tools go well beyond what VSTRK offers.
- **You are an enterprise team managing links across groups.** Multi-user controls on higher plans suit that.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is VSTRK a Bitly alternative?

For lead and revenue tracking, yes. For link management, no. Bitly focuses on creating, branding and measuring links, including QR codes. VSTRK focuses on connecting content and promoters to leads, calls, purchases and revenue. Many teams need one or the other, and some use both.

### Can Bitly track leads?

Bitly tracks link clicks and QR code scans. Bitly's analytics documentation says Bitly Analytics does not track conversions. You can follow leads by tagging links with UTM parameters and reading the results in an analytics tool or CRM, or by connecting Bitly to other tools through integrations.

### Can Bitly track purchases and revenue?

Not natively. Purchases and revenue are captured in your web analytics, ecommerce or payment tools, and UTM tags can connect link traffic to them. VSTRK records purchases against content and promoter links and supports Stripe integration for revenue.

### Does Bitly integrate with Stripe?

We did not find a native Stripe integration in the Bitly materials we reviewed. Some teams connect Bitly data to other tools through Zapier or analytics, but check Bitly's current integrations before relying on it.

### Can I track individual videos with Bitly?

Yes, for clicks. Create a separate short link for each video and compare clicks, referrers and devices. To see leads or revenue per video, you also need UTM tags, an analytics tool and connected payment data. In VSTRK each piece of content gets its own tracking link, with conversions and revenue reported against it.

### Can I track different affiliates or promoters with Bitly?

You can create a separate link for each promoter and compare clicks. That is a workaround rather than a dedicated partner feature. VSTRK includes partner tracking: multiple promoters can promote the same asset through separate tracking links, and you compare their results.

### Does VSTRK have QR codes?

QR code creation is not part of what we can confirm about VSTRK. If QR codes are central to your campaign, Bitly is the stronger choice.

### Does VSTRK do geographic and device analytics?

We have not established that it does. Bitly reports geography, devices and referrers, and is the better fit if those breakdowns matter to you.

### Which platforms does VSTRK track?

Instagram, X, YouTube, LinkedIn, Facebook, Reddit, TikTok, Threads and Twitch.

### Does VSTRK host or distribute my videos?

No. VSTRK provides tracking links. You publish and share your videos through your own channels.

### Can I use Bitly and VSTRK together?
